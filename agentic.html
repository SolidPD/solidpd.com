The Bottleneck

The hardest problems in a hardware product live at the boundary between firmware and physics.

A simulator won't catch the charge controller that only fails when a thermistor is disconnected. A code review won't find the sensor that never sleeps and quietly drains your battery. These bugs only reveal themselves on real hardware, under live conditions — and finding them takes an engineer, a bench full of instruments, and time: flash, measure, think, adjust, repeat. That loop is slow, expensive, and it doesn't scale. It is the quiet bottleneck that slows down almost every hardware program, and the scope that quietly gets cut when time runs out.

We Closed the Loop — In-House

For the past two years, Solid has been building something we believe is genuinely new, built entirely by our own engineering team for our own clients: a system that puts AI agents inside the develop-test loop, not just beside it. It isn't a platform we license from anyone — it's technology our engineers designed, from the ground up, so that Solid can do more of the hardware engineering work we've always done, in less calendar time.

Our agents don't stop at writing firmware. They flash it to the actual device, drive lab instruments — programmable power supplies and loads, power profilers, waveform generators, logic and protocol analyzers — and read back measurements, including continuous visual and thermal camera coverage of the board itself. Then they form a hypothesis, change one thing, and measure again. And again. Around the clock, without fatigue, with a senior engineer directing the work and reviewing every result.

Write → Flash → Measure → Analyze ↻ repeat — around the clock, on real hardware

We run this on our own self-contained, remotely operable test cells: your device is fixtured once inside an instrumented enclosure, and from that moment the full test capability of the bench — flashing, power cycling, probing, imaging — is available on demand, from anywhere, in parallel across as many devices as the problem needs. One engineer, many benches — which is how we complete more of your scope in the same calendar time.

Where This Lets Us Move Fastest For You

This capability is ideal for firmware problems in hardware devices — and that's by design. Firmware iterates in seconds, its effects are physically measurable (current draw, timing, temperature, RF behavior), and the hardware itself is the objective referee: a fix either holds on the bench or it doesn't. That makes firmware the perfect material for a closed loop that can run thousands of build-flash-measure iterations while your team sleeps — so the engineering scope you need covered doesn't have to wait for the next available calendar slot.

Power Optimization

Battery life is a firmware problem wearing a hardware costume. Our agents profile current draw correlated line-by-line with firmware execution, find the waste — wake locks, chatty polling, radios that never rest, peripherals left powered — then implement the fixes and verify every milliamp of improvement on the physical device.

You get back a battery-life number, and the evidence trail behind it.

Bug Identification & Troubleshooting

The intermittent fault. The protocol handshake that fails one time in fifty. The inherited codebase nobody fully understands anymore. Our agents reproduce the failure on the device itself, instrument the code, bisect the behavior, and watch the result on the instruments — logic analyzer, power profiler, thermal camera — until the root cause has nowhere left to hide.

Then they prove the fix by trying to break it.

Regression & Endurance Testing

Fixture your board once, and your full hardware test suite re-runs on demand or on a schedule — against real rails, sensors, and loads. Soak tests, characterization sweeps, and overnight captures run unattended.

The regressions simulators miss get caught before they ship.

Why You Can Trust It

AI with its hands on hardware raises fair questions. We designed for them from day one.

Safety

Enforced Below the AI

The agent never touches an instrument directly — it can only ask. A policy layer owns every instrument handle and refuses or clamps any request outside the device's safety envelope. Below that sits a non-overridable hardware safety floor: absolute thermal ceilings, smoke-triggered power cutoff, an e-stop chain, and a watchdog that fails to a safe state even if everything above it is dead.

Evidence

Measurements, Not Claims

Every measurement writes a durable, append-only record: timestamped raw data, the exact intent that requested it, the device profile it ran under, a safety-state snapshot, and camera frames of the board at capture time. Refusals are logged too.

Every number in our reports traces back to who asked, what ran, and what the device looked like when it happened.

Accountability

An Engineer in Command

The loop is agentic, not autonomous-and-unaccountable. A senior engineer scopes the work, watches the same live session the AI runs, and stands behind every deliverable — the same way Solid stands behind any engineering work we deliver.
